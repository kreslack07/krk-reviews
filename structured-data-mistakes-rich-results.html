Structured Data Mistakes That Cost Small Businesses Their Rich Results

Published 2026-11-23 · By KRK Digital Products (ABN 71 770 320 895, Australia)

Rich results are a privilege, not a setting

Structured data does not make a page rank. Google's general structured data guidelines are blunt about it: valid markup makes a page eligible for an enhanced result, every rich-result feature carries its own required properties, and Google does not guarantee a rich result even when the markup is technically perfect.

The markup can also work the other way — it can remove eligibility. A structured data issue can result in a manual action, which means the affected pages lose their rich-result eligibility. The same guidelines note that a structured data manual action does not affect how the page ranks in web search. That is why the problem is easy to miss: the page still ranks, the stars, prices or FAQ dropdowns just quietly stop appearing.

Mistake 1: review stars for your own business

This is the most common one on small business sites. The review snippet guidelines state that when the entity being reviewed controls the reviews about itself, its pages using LocalBusiness or any other Organization type are ineligible for the star review feature — including reviews delivered through an embedded third-party widget. Marking up your own rating, or pulling your Google reviews into your own page markup, is exactly that case.

The same page also requires that ratings are sourced directly from users, that you don't aggregate reviews or ratings from other websites, and that incentivised reviews are clearly and prominently disclosed. Stars you didn't earn don't just fail to show. They can cost the page its whole rich-result set.

Mistake 2: markup for things people can't see

Don't mark up content that isn't visible to readers. If the JSON-LD describes a service, a price or a person, the page body has to describe the same thing in the same words a customer would read. The guidelines call the required relationship a true representation of the page content, and list irrelevant markup — one industry's format used on another industry's page — as a violation.

Mistake 3: blocked pages and missing properties

Invisible to tools, easy to fix once you know: don't block the marked-up page from Googlebot with robots.txt, noindex or a login. On completeness, items missing a required property are simply not eligible, and supplying the recommended properties is what raises the quality of the result. Supply current information too — Google won't show a rich result for time-sensitive content that is no longer relevant — and use the most specific schema.org type that fits.

Testing it without a developer

  1. Rich Results Test — validates the markup and flags critical errors.
  2. URL Inspection tool — shows how Google sees the rendered page, which is where blocked or hidden markup surfaces.
  3. Manual Actions report in Search Console — the only place a structured data manual action will be admitted to you.

One more habit worth keeping: put the markup on the page it describes, and if duplicate pages exist, use the same structured data on the duplicates as well as the canonical.
