The four questions the audit is built to answer

  1. Can search engines and AI answer engines reach and read your pages? A page a crawler is blocked from, or one that renders its content through client-side JavaScript, cannot be indexed or cited.
  2. Does each commercial page describe one service, to one buyer, in one place? Pages that mix four services, or that name no location, are hard for a search engine to match to a query.
  3. Does the structured data on your site match what the pages sell? Stale or deprecated markup tells search engines something different from what your visitors read.
  4. What is slowing the pages down? Oversized images, render-blocking scripts and layout shift all affect how a page is judged.

The report answers those four in that order, because that is the order in which the fixes pay off.

The four checks inside the audit

1. Technical crawl and indexation

  • robots.txt rules, and whether your firewall or security plugin is blocking search and AI retrieval crawlers alongside bad bots.
  • XML sitemap hygiene: every listed URL returning a successful response, canonical tags matching the sitemap, stale or redirected URLs removed.
  • HTTP status codes across your key pages, and redirect chains that waste crawl attention.
  • AI retrieval access for Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot, and whether any are shut out by a blanket bot rule.

2. On-page SEO and buyer intent

  • Replacement title tags, written to fit the visible width of a search result.
  • Replacement meta descriptions that state the deliverable, the turnaround and the currency of the price.
  • A single H1 per page, with H2 and H3 headings that name the service and the location rather than acting as decoration.
  • Internal links: which pages carry link equity, which important pages are orphaned, and which anchor text is generic.
  • Above-the-fold clarity: can a visitor tell what you sell, where you operate and what it costs without scrolling?

3. Schema.org and AI search readiness

  • Validation of your existing JSON-LD against current Google structured data rules, including markup types that have been deprecated or restricted.
  • Paste-ready Organization, Service or Product JSON-LD that describes your business as it operates today.
  • Consistency between your structured data, your visible page copy and your business listings.

4. Core Web Vitals and speed bottlenecks

  • The three Core Web Vitals metrics (LCP, CLS and INP) measured on the pages that matter commercially, and explained in plain words: how long the main content takes to appear, how much the layout moves while it loads, and how long the page takes to respond to a tap.
  • The assets, scripts and layout rules responsible, named so your developer or site builder can act on them.
  • A practical order of operations, because fixing speed is usually a short list of changes applied once rather than a rebuild.

What arrives in your inbox

How to act on the report

If you maintain your own site:

  • Work top-down. Clear the high-priority block before anything else, because indexation and rendering problems make on-page work hard to measure.
  • Change one page at a time. Titles, meta descriptions and headings before internal links, then structured data.
  • Apply the speed items as a batch, since they usually share one root cause: an oversized hero image, a blocking script, or a layout rule.
  • After each change, inspect the URL in Google Search Console and request indexing. That is how you confirm the change was seen.

If a developer maintains your site:

  • Hand over the high-priority block as the opening ticket. Each finding names the page and the precise change, so it reads as a task list rather than a strategy document.
  • Keep the medium-priority block for the next maintenance window, and the low-priority block as a backlog.
  • Ask them to confirm the structured data validates after deployment, because JSON-LD edited by hand in a theme file breaks quietly.

What the audit is not

  • It is not a retainer, and it does not commit you to further work.
  • It does not include link building, outreach or a content rewrite.
  • It does not promise a ranking position. Search results depend on your competitors, your market and the demand for the terms you choose. What the audit gives you is a diagnosis, a fix list, and the knowledge of which faults were yours to fix.
  • It is written for one website. If you run several, order one audit per site so each report stays specific.
