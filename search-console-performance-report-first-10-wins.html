Ten SEO Wins Hidden in Your Search Console Performance Report

Published 2026-11-22 · By KRK Digital Products (ABN 71 770 320 895, Australia)

What the default view is actually showing you

Open the Performance report in Search Console and you get clicks and impressions for the past three months. Four metrics sit above the chart, and Google defines them precisely:

  • Clicks — the number of times a user clicked your site from Google Search results.
  • Impressions — how many times your site appeared in Search results.
  • CTR — the click count divided by the impression count.
  • Average position — the average position of the topmost result from your site.

That last definition is where people go wrong. Average position is not "where my page ranks". It is the average position of your best-placed result for each query, which is why a page sitting at position 4 can report 3.2.

Pick the dimension before you touch a page

The table groups data by the dimension on the tab you choose: Queries, Pages, Countries, Devices, Search appearance, or Dates. The aggregation differs: data grouped by Queries, Countries, Devices or Dates is aggregated by property, while Pages and Search appearance are aggregated by page. That is also why the chart totals and the table totals can disagree — a known difference in aggregation, not a bug.

A five-step triage that produces ten real jobs

  1. Queries, sorted by impressions, CTR under 2%. These are searches where you are seen but not chosen. Rewrite the title and meta description for the page that ranks for each one.
  2. Queries sitting in positions 8–20 with steady impressions. Close to page one and worth real work: on-page copy, internal links from your strongest pages, and better heading structure.
  3. Impressions on words you never targeted. Google is telling you what your pages are already associated with. Either build the page that serves that intent properly, or tighten the copy so you stop attracting the wrong click.
  4. Pages tab, compared against your site average CTR. Isolate the pages whose snippets underperform, then check whether the search engine is rewriting your title link.
  5. Devices tab. If mobile CTR trails desktop on the same queries, the problem is mobile — tap targets, font size, or slow load — not your copy.

Three numbers that will look wrong, and aren't

The newest data can be preliminary — still being collected, shown with a dotted line on the chart — so today's dip may vanish by tomorrow. Chart totals count impressions per property, not per page: two results from your site on one query are a single chart impression. And "even if a query appears in your list, you might not see your site in results" if you run that search yourself, because results depend on your time, place, device and recent history.

Export it, then keep a dated record

Use the export button. Values shown as ~ or - (unavailable) land as zeros in the download, so quiet weeks look like real zeros — worth knowing before you build a trend from the file. Save the CSV with the date in the filename. Three or four months of saved exports turns "traffic feels down" into a comparison you can act on, and it is the fastest way to catch a page that is sliding before it drops off page one.
