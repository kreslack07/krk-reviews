Why AI Search Skips Your Small-Business Website — and What to Check

Published 2026-11-21 · By KRK Digital Products (ABN 71 770 320 895, Australia)

ChatGPT Search, Claude, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews answer questions by retrieving pages, reading them, and quoting them with a link. When your site is absent from those answers, one of those three steps has failed. Working out which one is a short diagnostic job, and it can be done from your own browser.

This page walks through the checks and names the fix at each step.

How an answer engine decides whether to cite you

Retrieval. A crawler from the answer engine requests your page. If robots.txt, a firewall rule or a login wall stops it, nothing further happens: the page cannot appear in an answer, however good it is.

Reading. The crawler parses the response and works out what the page is about. Text is quotable. Content locked inside an image, a PDF attachment, a form field or a widget that needs JavaScript to load is not.

Citation. The engine compares what it read against the question. A page that states one service, one location and one price is easy to match to a query. A page that lists four services, names no location and hides its pricing is hard to match to anything.

Check 1: can the retrieval crawler reach the page?

Start with your robots.txt file. A blanket Disallow rule written to stop scrapers also stops the crawlers that feed AI search. The retrieval agents worth naming explicitly are:

  • Googlebot — Google Search and Google AI Overviews.
  • Bingbot — Microsoft Bing and its syndicated partners.
  • OAI-SearchBot — OpenAI's search retrieval crawler for ChatGPT Search.
  • ClaudeBot — Anthropic's retrieval crawler for Claude answers.
  • PerplexityBot — Perplexity's indexing crawler for real-time citation.

Security plugins and host-level bot rules are the next place to look, because they often block on user-agent strings before robots.txt is read. Test by fetching a page with each user agent, or ask your host to confirm the rule set.

While you are there, check that no page you care about carries a noindex tag, and that your sitemap file lists live URLs.

Check 2: is the fact written down as text?

An answer engine can quote a sentence. It cannot quote a picture of a price list.

For each of these facts, ask whether a machine could read it from your page as plain text:

  • What you sell, named the way a customer would search for it.
  • The price, written with its currency — the currency code removes any doubt about which market you serve.
  • The turnaround time or availability, if you publish one.
  • The suburbs, cities or regions you serve.
  • How to contact you, as text and as a link, rather than inside an image.

If your pricing sits in a graphic, or your service area lives in a footer logo, move the facts into the body copy. Keep the design; add the sentence.

Check 3: does your structured data describe the business you actually run?

JSON-LD is how you tell a search engine what your page is, in a format it does not have to guess at. Two things go wrong here.

Deprecated or restricted markup. Structured data types that were once encouraged have been retired or narrowed, and a page relying on them reads as though it has no structured data at all. Validate each template on your site against the current Google structured data documentation rather than trusting a plugin settings screen.

Markup that contradicts the page. An Organization block naming a former business name, an old phone number, a closed location or a currency you no longer charge in will follow you into search results and into AI answers. Bring the markup in line with the copy, and keep your business listings consistent with both.

Check 4: does each page answer a single question?

Retrieval works better against pages that are clearly about one thing.

  • One service per page. A page offering bookkeeping, payroll and tax returns competes with itself in the index.
  • One H1 per page, followed by H2 and H3 headings that name the service and the location.
  • Location written into the page, not just the title tag — a suburb name in the body copy is what matches a local query.
  • Duplicates resolved. If two URLs serve the same content, choose one, point a canonical tag at it, and redirect the other.
  • Thin pages retired. A page created for a service you no longer offer dilutes the site. Remove it and redirect, or rewrite it.

Check 5: does the page render quickly enough to be retrieved?

Speed affects retrieval in a practical way: a crawler has a limited budget for how long it waits.

  • How long the main content takes to appear. Usually a hero image or a font file. Compress the image, serve modern formats, and preload the font you actually use.
  • How much the layout moves while it loads. Usually a banner or an ad slot inserted without reserved space.
  • How long the page takes to respond to a tap. Usually a script that blocks the main thread on load.

If your content arrives after a client-side script runs, treat that as a retrieval risk as well as a speed one. Server-rendered or statically generated pages are easier for every crawler to read.

A 20-minute self-check you can run today

  1. Open your robots.txt at your domain root and read it line by line.
  2. Fetch your home page and one service page with a crawler user agent, or ask your host to confirm the retrieval agents are allowed.
  3. View your page source and search for noindex, and for application/ld+json.
  4. Read your home page on a phone with the images turned off. If the meaning disappears, the facts are in the images.
  5. Search your own domain name in ChatGPT Search, Claude and Perplexity, and see whether your site is quoted and which page is used.
  6. Inspect three key URLs in Google Search Console and confirm they can be indexed.
  7. Run your home page through PageSpeed Insights and note the three named diagnostics rather than the score.

Anything that fails at steps 1 to 4 is a structural problem, and worth fixing before the rest. Anything that fails at steps 5 to 7 is measurable, and the figures tell you where the time goes.
