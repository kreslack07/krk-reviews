AI Crawlers in robots.txt: Which Ones to Allow So Assistants Cite You

Published 2026-11-22 · By KRK Digital Products (ABN 71 770 320 895, Australia)

“Block the AI bots” is the wrong instruction

Every assistant vendor splits its traffic into separate robots.txt tokens, and each token does a different job. Block them all and you drop out of the answer boxes that now sit above your organic listing. Block none and you hand over training data you may not want to give away. The tokens are published by each vendor, so this is a reading exercise, not a guess.

Three categories, per Google's own crawler documentation

Google's crawling documentation groups its clients into common crawlers (they “always respect robots.txt rules” for automatic crawls), special-case crawlers used by specific products, and user-triggered fetchers that act because a person asked for something. The same split exists at OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity. The category matters more than the vendor name.

The token that is not a crawler at all

Google-Extended has no HTTP user agent of its own — Google crawls with its existing agents and reads the token in a control capacity. It governs whether content Google already crawls may be used to train future Gemini models and to ground Gemini Apps and Vertex AI. Google states plainly that it “does not impact a site's inclusion in Google Search nor is it used as a ranking signal in Google Search”.

OpenAI: three tags, three decisions

  • OAI-SearchBot — search. Sites opted out “will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though can still appear as navigational links”. Allow it if you want citations. OpenAI says allow roughly 24 hours after a robots.txt change.
  • GPTBot — training. Disallowing it “indicates a site's content should not be used in training generative AI foundation models”.
  • ChatGPT-User — user-initiated fetches. Because a user asked, “robots.txt rules may not apply”, and OpenAI says this token is not the one that controls Search.

Anthropic and Perplexity

Anthropic publishes ClaudeBot (training), Claude-User (user-requested fetches) and Claude-SearchBot (search relevance), and states its bots “respect do not crawl signals by honoring industry standard directives in robots.txt”. It also supports the non-standard Crawl-delay directive, and asks that you opt out on every subdomain separately. Perplexity's PerplexityBot is for surfacing and linking sites in search results and is not used to crawl for foundation models; Perplexity-User handles user-requested fetches and, in Perplexity's words, “generally ignores robots.txt rules”. Both vendors publish IP ranges, which is what you allowlist if a WAF is blocking them.

A starter block

# Let answer engines find and cite the site
User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /
User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /
User-agent: Claude-SearchBot
Allow: /

# Opt out of model training
User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /
User-agent: ClaudeBot
Disallow: /
User-agent: Google-Extended
Disallow: /

What a robots.txt line cannot do

It cannot remove content already collected, and user-triggered fetchers may ignore it by design. It also does nothing if a CDN or firewall blocks the bot before robots.txt is ever read — the vendor IP lists exist for exactly that reason.
