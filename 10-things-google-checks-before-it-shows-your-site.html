The 10 Things Google Checks Before It Shows Your Site

A polished website does not guarantee local customers will find you online. Before Google shows your business in search results, its automated crawlers inspect these ten essential signals.

  1. Mobile Readiness: Most local searches happen on phones, so Google demotes sites that force visitors to pinch or scroll sideways. Every page adjusts smoothly to any screen with readable text and easy-to-tap buttons.
  2. Fast Loading Speed: Slow pages push impatient buyers to leave early, signaling to Google that your website delivers a poor experience. Your main content loads in under two seconds on mobile using compressed images.
  3. Secure HTTPS Connection: Google warns visitors away from unsecured sites to protect their privacy and keep customer inquiries safe from interception. Every page loads automatically over a valid security certificate and shows the browser lock icon.
  4. Hidden Indexing Blocks: Developers often leave a hidden `noindex` tag or robots block active after launch, silently telling Google to ignore your entire website. Your page code and crawler rules openly invite search engines to index every public page.
  5. Clear Page Titles and Summaries: Search engines use title tags and meta descriptions to understand what you sell and preview your page in search results. Each key page has a unique, plain-English title and summary stating your service and location.
  6. Logical Heading Structure: Google scans your headings like a newspaper outline to confirm your page directly answers what the customer searched for. Every page uses one main heading for your core offer and clear subheadings for the details.
  7. Clean Internal Links: Broken links and unlinked pages trap crawlers in dead ends and keep your best service pages from being found. Every menu item, button, and link connects cleanly to a live page within two clicks of your homepage.
  8. Single Canonical Address: When your site loads both with and without `www`, Google can treat those versions as duplicate websites and split your ranking power. All address variations redirect automatically to one primary domain so your search authority stays unified.
  9. Consistent Local Business Signals: Google cross-checks your contact details to confirm you run an active, legitimate business before recommending you to local buyers. Your business name, address, phone number, and hours match across your site and Google Business Profile.
  10. Up-to-Date XML Sitemap: Without a clean roadmap of your site, Google can miss new pages or waste time scanning old drafts you deleted. An automated XML sitemap lists only your live, valuable pages and updates whenever you publish changes.
