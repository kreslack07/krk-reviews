Google Business Profile: The Local Fixes That Move the Needle

Published 2026-11-21 · By KRK Digital Products (ABN 71 770 320 895, Australia)

Your website is half of a local search presence. The other half is the business profile — the listing that answers location queries and, increasingly, supplies the facts an AI assistant repeats when someone asks for a recommendation in a suburb. Google publishes its own guidance on improving local ranking and on representing your business. Read those two pages; most other local SEO writing is a restatement of them.

This is the local half of our 48-hour AI & SEO website audit, written so you can work through it yourself.

Relevance, distance, prominence

Google names three factors behind local results: how well your profile matches what was searched, how far the business is from the searcher, and how well known it is. Distance is fixed. The other two are work.

Relevance begins with the category

Choose the most specific primary category that describes the main thing you sell, then add secondary categories just for services you genuinely offer. Fill in the services and products list, with names and starting prices where you are willing to publish them. Complete the attributes — appointment links, payment types, accessibility, parking — because those answer structured questions directly, for people and for machines.

Use the real-world business name

Google's guidelines ask for the name the business actually trades under. A name stuffed with suburbs and service keywords breaches those guidelines and puts the listing at risk. Put the keywords in the services, the description and your website copy instead, where they belong.

Prominence is earned partly off-site

Reviews carry weight, and the routine is unglamorous: ask after the work is done, reply to every review, and mention the service and the suburb naturally in your replies. Add fresh photos monthly — exterior, team, finished work — so the listing looks maintained. A short post each week gives the profile something current to show and gives the machine something recent to read.

Consistency between the site and the profile

Phone, address, hours and business name must match what your site's schema markup and footer say, and what the directories say. Mismatch creates doubt for a searcher, and it also weakens the entity matching that AI answers depend on. One number everywhere, or the listing competes with your own website.

Track it, then fix what is failing

The Performance tab reports calls, direction requests, website clicks and the searches that surfaced you. Read it month to month rather than week to week — local volumes are small and noisy, and a quiet Tuesday is not a trend.

The half that is easy to miss

A well-kept profile wins the click. The website has to hold it: fast on a phone, crawlable, marked up, and quoting the same details as the listing. When those two disagree, you pay for the traffic and still lose the job.
